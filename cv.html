Research Experience

Research Assistant

May 2024 – Aug 2026

Functional Materials and Optoelectronic Devices (FMOD) Lab, LUMS, Lahore, Pakistan

  • Supervisor: Dr. Ammar Ahmed Khan
  • Project: Fabrication and Time-Dependent Analysis of Polymer Photodetectors
  • Fabrication: Fabricated mesoscopic photodetectors (FTO/compact TiO2/mesoporous TiO2/polymer/Cr-Au) by spin coating, using P3HT, the P3HT–PDI physical hybrid (P3HT-PDI-PH), or the covalent donor–acceptor conjugate PTh-PDI-DCP as the active layer; deposited Cr-Au contacts by thermal evaporation.
  • Characterization: Measured transient photocurrent under 0.33 Hz chopped light at 0 V bias with a transimpedance amplifier; extracted 10–90% rise and fall times by curve fitting.
  • Results: PTh-PDI-DCP gave the shortest rise time (109 ± 3 ms), compared with P3HT-PDI-PH (133 ± 2 ms) and P3HT (172 ± 37 ms), while fall times did not show this speed-up; the fall times of PTh-PDI-DCP and P3HT-PDI-PH were longer than their rise times. The paper hypothesizes that the covalent bridge speeds charge transport from donor to acceptor, giving faster injection into the TiO2 scaffold. Published in [1] (Fig. 3).

Graduate Research (MS Thesis)

June 2023 – May 2024

Functional Materials and Optoelectronic Devices (FMOD) Lab, LUMS, Lahore, Pakistan

  • Supervisor: Dr. Ammar Ahmed Khan
  • Title: A Photodetector Based on Lead-Free Perovskite-like Methylammonium Bismuth Iodide (MBI)
  • Fabrication: With a team, grew MA3Bi2I9 (MBI) thin films by one-step spin coating from a methylammonium iodide/bismuth iodide precursor, varying the chlorobenzene anti-solvent from 20 to 175 µL·cm−2 to control nucleation. Independently fabricated mesoscopic photodetectors (FTO/compact TiO2/mesoporous TiO2/MBI/Cr-Au) at 90, 60, and 45 µL·cm−2.
  • Characterization: Analyzed SEM and XRD data to confirm the hexagonal MBI phase (space group P63/mmc) and quantify grain-size trends; designed the photodetector test assembly and measured device response under chopped white light (0 V bias, transimpedance-amplifier readout).
  • Results: The amount of anti-solvent tuned the average grain size by about 26-fold (1.40 ± 0.03 to 36 ± 1 µm). Device performance peaked at the intermediate anti-solvent concentration: reducing the anti-solvent from 90 to 60 µL·cm−2 raised peak photocurrent from about 2.6 to 7 µA·cm−2 and gave the fastest rise time (0.35 s), while 45 µL·cm−2 lowered photocurrent, attributed to larger but more sparsely spread grains. Published in [2] (Fig. 2, Table 1, and Fig. S2).

Bachelor’s Thesis Research (external thesis placement)

July 2021 – June 2022

Physical Vapor Deposition Lab, Center for Advanced Studies in Physics (CASP), Government College University (GCU), Lahore, Pakistan

  • Supervisor: Hira Yaseen
  • Title: Influence of Repetition Rate on the Growth of MgO Thin Film by Pulsed Laser Deposition Technique
  • Fabrication: Operated a Q-switched Nd:YAG pulsed laser deposition system (1064 nm, 200 mJ pulse energy, 10 ns pulse width) to grow MgO thin films on silicon substrates under vacuum (10−3 Torr), varying the laser repetition rate (2, 3, 4, and 5 Hz), with 2000 to 5000 total laser shots.
  • Characterization: Analyzed XRD, SEM, and AFM data to determine film thickness, crystallite size (Scherrer method), and grain morphology at each repetition rate.
  • Results: Film thickness rose from 307 nm (2000 shots) to 960 nm (5000 shots), while crystallite size fell from 65 to 15 nm.
