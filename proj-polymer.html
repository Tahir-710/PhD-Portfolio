Polymer photodetectors: covalent donor–acceptor bridging and photoresponse speed

Published in

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 17(19), 28609–28621 (2025)

10.1021/acsami.4c22745 ↗

Supervisor: Dr. Ammar Ahmed Khan

Why this project. Double-cable polymers join an electron donor (polythiophene) and an acceptor (perylene diimide) with a covalent bond. Chemistry collaborators made PTh-PDI-DCP as a photocatalyst; my job was to test whether that covalent link also changes how fast a device built from it responds to light, compared with the donor alone (P3HT) and their physical hybrid (P3HT–PDI-PH).

What I did

Fabricated mesoscopic polymer photodetectors on FTO: compact TiO₂ ETL, 300 nm mesoporous TiO₂ scaffold, and a capping layer of P3HT, P3HT–PDI-PH or PTh-PDI-DCP, with Cr–Au contacts (3 nm Cr / 70 nm Au) by thermal evaporation through a 9 mm² shadow mask.

Built the chopped-light optical setup and recorded time-dependent photocurrent under 0.33 Hz chopped white light at 0 V bias, through a transimpedance amplifier at 10⁶ V/A gain, extracting rise and fall times from the 10–90% transition.

What I found

PTh-PDI-DCP responded fastest at 109±3 ms: a 37% improvement over P3HT (172±37 ms) and 18% over P3HT–PDI-PH (133±2 ms). The speed-up did not carry over to switch-off. For PTh-PDI-DCP and P3HT–PDI-PH, the fall time was longer than the rise time.

Our reading, stated as a hypothesis in the paper: the covalent bridge speeds charge transport from donor to acceptor, giving faster injection into the TiO₂ scaffold. The paper also notes that the absolute times are device properties, set by several interfaces (polymer–TiO₂, TiO₂–FTO and FTO–contact); charge transfer itself is probed separately at nanosecond time scales with fluorescence and TRPL.
