Lead-free MA3Bi2I9 perovskite: anti-solvent engineering and photodetector performance

Published in

Advanced Engineering Materials 27(5), 2402009 (2025)

10.1002/adem.202402009 ↗

Supervisor: Dr. Ammar Ahmed Khan

Why this project. Lead in perovskites is a potential health and environmental hazard. Bismuth-based perovskites such as MA₃Bi₂I₉ are stable in ambient conditions, but their optoelectronic performance lags behind, partly because of poor film crystallinity and morphology. Adding an anti-solvent during spin coating changes how the crystals nucleate. The question: how does the amount of anti-solvent set grain size, and what grain size does a photodetector actually want?

What I did

With the lab team, grew lead-free MA₃Bi₂I₉ thin films by one-step spin coating from MAI/BiI₃ in DMSO:DMF (1:4), sweeping chlorobenzene anti-solvent concentration from 175 down to 20 µL·cm⁻² to tune crystal nucleation.

Fabricated MBI photodetectors in a mesoscopic n–i–p architecture (FTO / c-TiO₂ / mp-TiO₂ / MBI / Cr–Au) at three anti-solvent concentrations. independent

Designed the photodetector test assembly and measured photoresponse under ≈330 mW·cm⁻² chopped white light at 0 V bias.

Analyzed SEM images across the concentration series to quantify grain size, and XRD data to confirm the MA₃Bi₂I₉ phase.

What I found

Anti-solvent concentration tuned average grain size nearly 26-fold (1.40±0.03 to 36±1 µm). XRD matched the hexagonal MA₃Bi₂I₉ phase (space group P6₃/mmc), and films made with 175 and 45 µL·cm⁻² of anti-solvent showed the same characteristic peaks with no new ones.

Photocurrent depended on the anti-solvent concentration non-monotonically. Reducing anti-solvent from 90 to 60 µL·cm⁻² raised peak photocurrent from ≈2.6 to ≈7 µA·cm⁻² with a 0.35 s rise time; reducing further to 45 µL·cm⁻² brought it back to ≈3.8 µA·cm⁻². The paper attributes the rise to larger crystals and the drop at 45 µL·cm⁻² to larger grains that are spaced out.
